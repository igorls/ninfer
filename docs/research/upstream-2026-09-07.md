## Completed findings and qualification

### Flash-Next

**Defer upstream RMSNorm `9954867a` for Flash-Next performance.** The changed D=2,560 MTP
embedding route accounts for only 1.19–1.30 ms across complete short/long decode ranges in the
retained September 6 traces, or 0.09%/0.06% of summed kernel time. Those traces predate the
selected-block attention improvement. The D=10,240 hidden norm uses the generic kernel, which
the commit does not change; its gated 170-SM crossover is not reached by Flash-Next. This small
absolute cost does not justify a full Engine experiment. Dense RMSNorm remains a separate
candidate if its actual call sites have sufficient measured cost.

**The half-width MoE gate/up staging change is qualified for integration.** It stages two
K=1,280 tiles while retaining the original forty K64 MMA accumulation steps. Dynamic shared
memory falls from 92,416 to 46,336 bytes, registers from 106 to 57, with no spills. CUDA's
occupancy query permits two resident 256-thread CTAs instead of one; achieved occupancy was
not measured. This is a Flash-specific implementation, not an upstream cherry-pick.

Qualification covers signed packed inputs and independently varied per-group scales, all
512 experts, uniform/skewed routing and T=512/513/2,048/8,192. Every routed output is written
and finite; all outputs match the original kernel bitwise. An independent FP64 decoded-input
oracle passes 256 selected outputs, and a no-op negative control fails the sentinel gate.
The actual-launcher MoE integration test passes. Median isolated kernel-time reductions range
from 4.8% to 18.5%; these are not request-level speedups.

Fresh baseline and candidate Engine builds use the same MSVC 19.51/CUDA 13.3.33 toolchain,
mixed artifact, BF16 output head, FP8 KV, BF16 GDN state, Vision allocations, CUDA graphs,
8,192-token chunks, 65,536-token context limit and 196,608-token KV pool. Capacity is eight,
with one active request. Ordinary decoding and MTP4 are qualified independently. An initial
comparison against the older production executable also shifted untouched decode throughput;
it is excluded from optimization attribution.

| Root workload | Baseline TTFT | Candidate TTFT | Mean reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ordinary, 3,952 tokens | 0.515–0.520 s | 0.505–0.508 s | 2.2% |

These ranges contain three observations, not confidence intervals. The primary production-mode

165.22 versus 165.02 committed tok/s; no decode improvement is attributed to this prefill change.
Ordinary decode varies slightly between processes, so its smaller TTFT difference needs caution.

All 60 qualification requests pass, including schema output and immediate continuations.
Text and MTP acceptance counts at every draft position match between paired builds.
Continuations reuse 30,275 of 30,561 prompt tokens and retain approximately 0.20–0.21 s TTFT;
there is no material reuse gain. This approximately 99.1% cached-token case is not the vendor's
90% condition. Text equality and schema validity establish regression observations, not legal
model quality.

### DFlash2 integration

The coherent DFlash2 dependency closure through `a16b6442` is integrated: converter and binder,
five-layer drafter and selector, variable-width Ops, target feature capture, cyclic state,
Engine scheduling, graphs, product options and measurement consumers. The original NVFP4
target payloads are preserved in a separate artifact containing the complete companion. The
artifact adds approximately 2.07 GiB of companion payload and 40 MiB per StateImage. Native
Windows startup and real execution are qualified on the RTX PRO 6000.

Qualification includes independent numerical Op oracles, graph and eager execution, K=7/K=15,
eight concurrent requests, mixed and all-constrained batches, partial terminals, cancellation,
page/ring boundaries, Host restore and image/video input. Both DFlash2 K=7 and MTP K=5 pass
all 21 live protocol/schema checks. A reproduced concurrent admission failure is fixed:
unfinished StateImage forks temporarily block admission instead of being offered to a pressure
plan that cannot seal. The scheduler regression and repeated real pressure runs pass.

An all-constrained batch now uses width-one target execution while preserving backend state.
A paired DFlash2 comparison improves structured JSON from 52.9 to 63.0 committed tokens/s;
ordinary prose throughput shows no clear regression. Mixed batches retain masked speculative
execution. Output text can differ across verification widths, so this integration is not
advertised as bitwise lossless. These checks do not establish Tribuno legal-answer quality.

Nsight Systems attributes 89.8% of decode kernel time to target verification and 8.5% to the
drafter/selector. Two GDN alternatives did not improve the identified operator and were reverted.
Nsight Compute counters remain unavailable (`ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM`); no counter-based occupancy or
bandwidth claim is made. The [performance reference](../performance.md) owns the matched serving
results and backend recommendations. At resident capacity eight, DFlash2 K7 reaches 176–196
committed tok/s with one active request, versus 159–184 with MTP5. At eight active requests,
MTP5 leads on Python/translation; mathematics is close. No single backend wins every workload.

## Remaining plan

1. **Target verification is the next dense optimization opportunity.** The DFlash2 integration
   and correctness gates are complete. Further kernel work should target the measured NVFP4
   gate/up and down projections or fused GDN input, and demonstrate a complete request gain.
   Reassess backend choice at the intended concurrency and context length before changing the
   production dense configuration. Larger draft blocks and a more expensive proposal head did
   not produce the best single-request result in the measured sweep.
2. **Resume Flash-Next profiling after the dense result.** Remaining attention, routed MoE,
   recurrent-state and output-head costs are candidates only where profiling can change a
   decision. Keep cold and reused prefixes separate, and measure intended active concurrency.
   The matched QSA comparison below remains pending. The 35B DFlash boundary fixture is a
   shared regression check when its exact companion is available, not a 35B tuning campaign.

The [`47bb19a5` TTFT staging fix](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer/commit/47bb19a5f715ef51c181f77f862dfe38758ecc78)
is included in the current benchmark tooling change: accept valid unaligned-size artifacts and
reject empty files. Its focused checks remain part of this integration; it is tooling correctness,
not a speed improvement.
The 35B-only [small-T router](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer/commit/487f89773f07cb18a2fb841fe0971ec9634d409b)
and [direct-input MoE staging](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer/commit/0a204aee3e8f8620377cd38d76621f9564507f3b)
remain deferred; Flash-Next's 512-expert/top-10 router does not call those implementations.

## Pending matched QSA/prefill comparison

The requested comparison against Qwen3.8-27B is **not yet performed**. The Qwen announcement's
growing prefill advantage under 90% prefix reuse is an unanswered measurement question on this
workstation. Earlier Flash profiles used roughly 30K cold prompts, inherited dense tables used
RTX 5090, and the Tribuno comparison used remote dense deployments.

With combined qualification and timing validation complete, compare Flash-Next mixed and Qwen3.8-27B
NVFP4 on the same Windows/RTX PRO 6000 with matched application source texts, applicable precision
policies, active concurrency and output budgets. Start at 16K, 64K, 128K and 256K total context,
restricted to lengths supported by both configurations. Consider 512K/1M only after checking
target contracts and memory capacity; never silently truncate inputs.

Measure cold requests and an explicit 90%-cached-token condition. Seed the prefix, append a
controlled suffix, and verify cached-token count and computed suffix for every observation.
Report tokenizer/template differences, achieved ratio and checkpoint rounding. A request-level
cache-hit percentage is not interchangeable with a cached-token ratio, and the vendor chart
does not establish identical hardware or cache semantics.

Report TTFT, actual uncached prefill work per second and complete request latency separately.
Use the qualified request-owned timer or explicit NVTX phase boundaries; historical logs with
a zero-valued Flash prefill field must not enter throughput calculations. Attribute attention
and total prefill independently where needed to distinguish QSA gains from whole-model gains.
Begin with speculation disabled, then add intended MTP settings as a separate configuration.
Keep cold preparation outside cached-suffix timing and rotate paired runs to expose drift.

The remaining work does not authorize an unconditional merge of upstream master or establish
legal-workflow model quality.
